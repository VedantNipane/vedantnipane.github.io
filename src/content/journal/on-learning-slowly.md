A year into the job, I've started to notice what I actually understand versus what I can convincingly repeat.

These are not the same thing, and the gap between them is where most of my anxiety lives.

In college you learn fast because the syllabus tells you what "done" looks like. At work there's no syllabus. The scope is undefined, the goalposts move, and the only signal you get is whether something ships or doesn't. You can spend a week on something and never really know if you understood it or just got it to work.

I've been thinking about this a lot while working on dataset pipelines. There's a version of the work where you follow the recipe: clean the data, run the script, check the numbers. And there's another version where you understand *why* the numbers should look a certain way, what you'd do if they didn't, and what the recipe is actually optimizing for.

The second version is slower. It requires sitting with confusion longer than feels comfortable. But it's the one that compounds.

I'm trying to do more of the second version. Not always succeeding. But trying.
