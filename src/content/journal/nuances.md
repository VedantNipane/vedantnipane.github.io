I have been trying to jot down my thoughts on this topic long enough to write a thesis on it, yet I still struggle to complete a single sentence.

In countless conversations, people advise: "Take a nuanced approach. Pay attention to the details. Only then will you see the whole picture."

And I've tried. A lot of times. And I have a bunch of thoughts. But my thoughts remain scattered, like fragments in the nooks and corners of my mind. Let's try to connect them and see if they make any sense.

### The Puzzle of Perspective

Take someone like me. In yearbook testimonials, people praised my writing, my discipline, and my teamwork. But none mentioned my fears — of water, heights, or speeding vehicles. I'm glad they didn't, but to truly sketch my character, you'd need to know not just my strengths but also my fears, my past, and the countless other things that shape me.

The same applies to socio-political issues. Consider India's ruling party, the BJP. Supporters and critics dominate the discourse, each emphasising details that align with their views. But most of us, whether we support, criticise, or stay neutral, form opinions based on headlines and algorithmic feeds. To really understand the BJP or any political party, we'd need to wade through its history, conflicts, ideologies, and more.

So, I hope we agree that details are important to get the whole picture. Cool.

### The Allure of Abstraction

Now there's another force at play: **abstraction**. It's about hiding unnecessary details to focus on the essentials. Think of a car - you don't need to understand thermodynamics to drive from Hyderabad to Warangal. You just need to know how to operate it.

Here's the catch: ever since I started digging into the nuances of national and international socio-political issues, my decision-making has taken a toll. I'm paralysed by the fear of missing some critical detail. And even when I do grasp the complexities, taking a side feels impossible — because every argument seems equally valid and equally flawed.

### The Dilemma: Nuance vs. Action

So, is nuance a virtue or a burden? Should we abstract things for convenience and move forward? The answer that I consider for now, frustratingly, is: It depends. There's a thin line between depth and paralysis. You must gauge the situation and decide for yourself.

But let's be honest - this is really messed up.

### Reflections from the Fog

Nuance feels like walking through fog: everything is there, but you need patience to make it out. Abstraction feels like standing on a hill — you see clearly, but from a distance.

The real challenge lies in knowing **when to zoom in and when to zoom out**. Not every decision demands a doctoral thesis. Sometimes, "good enough" is enough. Other times, like when voting or judging a movement, nuance isn't just helpful — it's ethical.

Yet, the weight of nuance can crush clarity. The more dimensions you consider, the harder it is to act. But perhaps that's the point: nuance isn't about finding perfect answers. It's about resisting the seduction of simplicity in a world that's anything but simple.

### The Tightrope Walk

In the end, the tension between nuance and abstraction mirrors life itself. Too much detail, and you're lost. Too little, and you're blind. The wisdom lies in balancing the two, knowing when to dissect and when to drive.

But damn, that balance is hard.

> If you take every side seriously, how do you ever take a stand?

That's the toll of nuance. It slows you down, weakens slogans, and delays judgment. But it also deepens your insight and reduces harm. Maybe the goal isn't to resolve the tension, but to learn to live with it.
